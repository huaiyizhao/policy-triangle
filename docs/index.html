The Policy Triangle A taxonomy of policy mismatch in LLM reinforcement learning

Rollouts are generated by one policy, scored against another, and used to update a third. The policy triangle turns that apparent mess into three edges and three operations.

The hard part is not that LLM RL has too many ratios. It is that the same ratio is often asked to do several different jobs: correct old data, reject bad samples, and constrain a new update.

01 · The mismatch problem

One rollout, three policy identities

In a small synchronous experiment, “the policy” can feel like one object. Production LLM reinforcement learning is different. A fast inference engine generates responses while a training engine performs several optimizer updates. Weight synchronization is delayed. Numerical kernels, precision, sampling transforms, or mixture-of-experts routing may differ.

As a result, three policy identities matter for every sampled token: the behavior policy that actually generated it, the proximal policy used as an update anchor, and the current policy receiving gradients.

Existing methods respond with importance sampling, ratio clipping, rejection, divergence masks, or sequence aggregation. These mechanisms are individually understandable, yet difficult to compare when described as unrelated losses. The triangle starts from policy identity instead.

The three ratios are not independent: the total behavior-to-current mismatch factors through the proximal policy, \(E = BU\).

\(B = q / \mu\) Already happened
\(U = \pi / q\) Current update drift
\(E = \pi / \mu = BU\) End-to-end mismatch

02 · Edge semantics

Every edge admits three operations

Once the policies are fixed, a method can be described by where it reads mismatch and what it does with that information. The distinction that matters most is whether gradients flow through the operation.

0/1

Mask · select

A detached predicate drops a token, sequence, or group. It may read any edge and any statistic: ratio bands, K2/K3, binary KL, full KL, policy age, or metadata.

No gradient · selection bias
×w

Weight · correct

A detached multiplier changes measure. Raw, truncated, tapered, and self-normalized importance sampling all live here. A ratio-carrying weight “consumes” its edge.

No gradient · variance control
∂

Shape · constrain

A differentiable operator changes the update itself. PPO clipping, asymmetric bounds, smooth gates, and divergence-based trust regions are shapers rather than preprocessing filters.

Gradient flows · update geometry

Geometry is a separate choice. A method may use token-level importance weights, a sequence-level rejection statistic, and a group-level normalization at the same time. “Token versus sequence” is not a global mode.

Bypass and decoupled optimization

Whether the proximal policy is distinct determines the topology, but it does not decide which masks or geometries are legal.

Bypass

Set \(q \equiv \mu\). The triangle collapses, so statistical correction and update control both refer to the direct edge. This saves a proximal forward pass but merges two causal roles.

\(B = 1,\quad U = E\)

Decoupled

Freeze a distinct proximal anchor \(q\). The behavior edge can be corrected with a detached weight while the current update is constrained against \(q\).

\(\mathrm{W}(B)\,\mathrm{C}(U)\)

03 · The accounting check

In the unshaped limit, consume exactly one copy of \(E\).

Set all masks to one, remove clipping and truncation, and ignore normalization. If the intended estimator changes measure from the behavior policy to the current policy, its coefficient should reduce to \(E = B U\)—not \(E^2\), and not a ratio with an edge missing.

✓ Direct
\(\mathrm{C}(E)\) or \(\mathrm{W}(E)\)
The total edge is consumed once.
✓ Factorized
\(\mathrm{W}(B)\,\mathrm{C}(U)\)
Behavior correction and update control factor \(E\).
✕ Double counted
\(\mathrm{W}(E)\,\mathrm{C}(E) \rightarrow E^2\)
A direct PPO ratio already carries \(E\).
✕ Mixed incorrectly
\(\mathrm{W}(E)\,\mathrm{C}(U) \rightarrow BU^2\)
The update edge is consumed twice.

This is bookkeeping, not a new convergence theorem. Clipping, masking, geometric sequence ratios, and self-normalization may still introduce intentional bias or approximation.

04 · Classification

Where familiar methods sit

The table is not a leaderboard. It isolates the policy-mismatch part of each method and asks which edge is read, which operator is applied, and at what geometry.

Method family Topology Triangle placement Interpretation
PPO / GRPO / DAPO Coupled \(\mathrm{C}_{tok}(E)\) The direct ratio carries correction and proximal-control roles; DAPO uses asymmetric bounds.
CISPO / TOPR Direct PG \(\mathrm{W}_{tok}(E)\) A detached clipped or tapered weight; gradients need not vanish outside a PPO band.
GSPO Coupled \(\mathrm{C}_{geo}(E)\) A length-normalized sequence ratio shapes the update; the original objective has no separate pre-filter.
Decoupled PPO / AReaL / A-3PO Factorized \(\mathrm{W}(B)\,\mathrm{C}(U)\) Detached behavior correction is separated from a trainable proximal constraint.
TIS / MIS / rollout correction Either \(\mathrm{M}(B \text{ or } E)\,\mathrm{W}(B \text{ or } E)\) Weighting and rejection compose; bypass versus decoupled changes which edge the same config denotes.
IcePop / KPop Usually direct \(\mathrm{M}_{tok}(E)\) + base loss IcePop uses a ratio region; KPop uses bidirectional binary-KL geometry.
DPPO / TRM Direct Divergence \(\mathrm{M}(E)\) or \(\mathrm{C}(E)\) Distributional geometry replaces sampled-ratio magnitude; TRM rejects a sequence using worst-token divergence.

  1. Bypass does not mean “importance sampling is off.”

    It means \(B=1\). A direct policy-gradient or PPO loss may still carry \(E=U\).

  2. PPO and GSPO clipping are not preprocessing filters.

    The clipped branch changes gradient flow in an advantage-dependent direction; a detached mask rejects before the loss.

  3. A filter may read a different edge from the weight below it.

    A sequence may be rejected using total mismatch \(E\) while the retained tokens are corrected only with \(B\) and shaped with \(U\).

05 · Relation to prior views

A common coordinate system, not a replacement

Decoupled PPO is the closest precursor: it already inserts a proximal policy to separate behavior correction from update control. AReaL and A-3PO adapt that factorization to asynchronous LLM training. The policy triangle is broader in operator placement, but shallower algorithmically: it does not prescribe the proximal anchor.

The veRL rollout-correction formulation and FP8-RL compare practical TIS, MIS, bypass, decoupled, and rejection configurations. The triangle abstracts that implementation pipeline and additionally places direct PPO, GSPO, and divergence-based update shapers in the same view.

RPG and the off-policy interpretation of group-relative REINFORCE give deeper objective-level analyses of KL regularization, baselines, and data shaping. This taxonomy is intentionally narrower: it focuses on the critic-free policy-loss mismatch path and leaves advantage estimation and reward regularization outside its scope.

06 · What the taxonomy exposes

Three research templates worth testing

A useful taxonomy should make empty cells visible. The following exact combinations were not found as named, evaluated methods in the literature reviewed through July 2026. They are hypotheses—not claims of priority or effectiveness.

Decoupled sequence shaping

\(\mathrm{W}_{tok}(B)\,\mathrm{C}_{geo}(U)\)

Correct rollout mismatch token by token, then constrain the current update with a sequence-geometric proximal ratio. This combines the attribution of decoupled PPO with the sequence coherence of GSPO.

Heterogeneous two-edge control

\(\mathrm{M}_{entropy}(B)\,\mathrm{W}(B)\,\mathrm{M}_{div}(U)\,\mathrm{C}(U)\)

Use entropy-aware admission on the behavior edge and a distributional trust-region statistic on the update edge. Backend noise and legitimate exploration no longer share one threshold.

Segment-aware async correction

\(\mathrm{M}_{seq}(E)\,\mathrm{W}_{segment}(B_t)\,\mathrm{C}(U)\)

For a partial rollout generated by several policy versions, normalize or truncate behavior weights within version-homogeneous segments instead of treating policy age as one trajectory-wide scalar.

These templates require controlled experiments before they should be interpreted as recommendations.

07 · An implementation audit

Five questions to ask before training

  1. 1
    What distribution actually generated each token? Include temperature, top-k/top-p transforms, backend precision, and piecewise policy versions.
  2. 2
    What is the proximal anchor? Is \(q\) the behavior policy, a frozen training snapshot, or an approximation such as A-3PO?
  3. 3
    For every operator, which edge and geometry does it read? Record detachment, token/sequence/group scope, statistic, sign rule, and normalization domain.
  4. 4
    Does the unshaped kernel contain exactly one \(E\)? Check the base loss together with any rollout-correction multiplier; configurable components can double count silently.
  5. 5
    Are raw tails measured before they are hidden? Log \(B\), \(U\), and \(E\), rejection, effective sample size, gradient variance, policy age, reward, and throughput.

What this framework does not claim

The triangle does not choose thresholds, guarantee that masking improves return, repair support mismatch, or replace empirical validation. It omits value/advantage off-policy correction, reward-model drift, environment nonstationarity, and optimizer-state staleness. Its main value is comparative: methods become easier to classify, audit, and combine without conflating selection, correction, and optimization.
